## What you'll see first

Open the artifact and set it to night (Daylight slider to 0), then drag the tide.

1. **The water finally glows like the reference.** Real bathymetry (see below) gives the
   ocean genuine structure — the ebb-tide delta at the harbour mouth renders its true
   lobes, the surf band hugs the beach, the deep goes navy by ~5 km out. Channels glow as
   ribbons because they are *actually deeper* — not because a distance-from-shore trick
   said so.
2. **Tauranga glows like a city now.** The old "city lights" were a landcover-albedo proxy
   — bare farmland outshone the CBD. Rebuilt from OSM roads/buildings weighted like real
   streetlighting, cross-lit by NASA VIIRS night-radiance so the large-scale distribution
   is real. CBD/Mount/Papamoa are the warm heart of the night view; arterials thread out
   as filaments; the countryside goes properly dark.
3. **The tide moves smoothly.** The popping zones are gone — measured, not vibes: the
   height raster was 8-bit (15.7 mm steps), so whole flats sat on one code and flipped at
   once (one plateau covered **41.8%** of a test flat). The field is now 16-bit
   (0.06 mm steps; largest plateau **5.2%**), decoded losslessly in the shader, dithered,
   and the damp band's hard edges are smoothstepped. A fine tide sweep now advances as a
   thin line every step.
4. **The channels flow.** A line-integral-convolution texture computed along the real
   channel directions gives the braided, swirling filaments from your reference image.
   It breathes on a 60-second cycle and the drift direction follows the tide — seaward on
   the ebb, landward on the flood. Slider: *Channel flow*.
5. **Imagery is richer.** New default basemap **Fused** = LINZ 0.1 m detail + Sentinel-2
   colour (pansharpen-style, with local exposure matching). The washed-out grey is gone;
   the forest corner you liked survives. Dropdown: Fused / LINZ original / fresh
   Sentinel-2 (new 3-scene median composite) / Fused+grade.
6. **Swell reads as water** — three feathered frequencies, noise-jittered, drifting
   shoreward, confined to true offshore. Whisper-level by default.

## The hard bug of the night

Every previous attempt at night glow drew **dark outlines around pools and creeks** — your
"lines around tide zones" complaint. Root cause (found by pixel-transect, after two wrong
guesses): in the band where the height raster climbs from the always-wet sentinel value to
fitted values, the glow formula consulted a tide-depth term that is clamped and meaningless
there, painting a navy ring around every permanent creek. Below the fitted range the height
says nothing about depth — the glow now consults real bathymetry only, blended on height
itself. Rims gone at every tide level. A second contributor: the night "pearl" on exposed
flats was strictly proportional to the aerial's luminance, and the photo is near-black
along wet margins — it now has a luminance floor plus a wet-margin sheen.

## On the AI-image-modifier idea

Parked, deliberately. The LIC flow field gets the reference's swirling-channel look
procedurally, deterministically, and in perfect registration with the real geometry — an
image model restyling one layer can't guarantee any of those three, and consistency across
tide states would be the killer. Where a generative pass *could* help later: a one-off
retouch of the basemap (cloud shadows, the misclassified vessel). If you want that, it
needs an image-editing API wired into the pipeline with the output diffed and masked, so
it can only touch what it's told to.
